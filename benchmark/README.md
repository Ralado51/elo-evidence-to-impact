# benchmark

The synthetic adversarial cases live in `api/benchmark/cases.json`, generated by
`api/benchmark/generate.py` (synthetic data only). The engine is `api/src/elo/benchmark.py`; run it with
`api/scripts/run_benchmark.py`.

- 69 cases, pt-BR and English, split `dev` / `eval` by a fixed hash. Tune rules and prompts on `dev` only; run `eval` once at the end.
- 46 easy cases (every verdict, negation, accents, mixed language) and 23 hard ones written to break the deterministic rules (goals, hypotheticals, another person, hand-guided help, two activities, cross-sentence).
- Labels come from how each case is constructed and are pending clinical review by the occupational therapist. A test checks that the labels agree with the verifier when extraction is perfect.
- Headline safety metrics: `unsafe_supported` (predicted supported, truth is not) and `false_contradicted`, plus a confusion matrix, extraction precision/recall and the abstentions.
- Baseline (rules, all splits): easy cases 100%, hard cases 0%; the hard failures are reported, not hidden.

## Rounds (generalization)

- **Round 1** hard cases (`hard-*`) were used to shape the prompt and the pack (exclusions for goals, hypotheticals, another person, failed attempts, hand guiding). Their results are optimistic.
- **Round 2** hard cases (`r2-*`) are new kinds of difficulty (almost achieved, temporal order, implicit setting, unlisted synonyms and report phrases, unlisted hand-guiding wording, claims dated before the note, progress over time). They were written after the tuning and nothing was changed for them. Look at `by_round` for the honest number.
- Known limits stay visible: report phrases outside the pack's cues (for example "segundo a avó") are read as direct observation by the rules; two activities in one sentence and level of help in a different sentence go to human review.
- Run the `eval` split once, at the end, with the rules and prompt frozen.
